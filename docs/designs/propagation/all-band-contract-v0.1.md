All A01–A24 are retained below as candidate obligations. A01–A05, A10–A13,
A15–A16 and A23–A24 contain normative candidate interfaces, domain boundaries
and verification requirements. Algorithm/source fidelity remains unverified.
A06–A09, A14 and A17–A22 contain experimental numerical/empirical models;
the channel products in A08 and mode expansion in A09 are schematic and do not
specify complete boundary conditions, coupling or a production solver. A03's
phasor and coherent-composition convention is normative candidate mathematics.
No family is validated by adopting these equations.

The machine-readable coverage rows in [the protocol](../../../ml/propagation_validation/protocol-v0.1.json)
are an initial required inventory, each with a stable band/mechanism/event/domain/horizon
identity and event-specific qualification metric. Shared data/power gates require
row-specific preregistration; the SNR 20% MAE target is never applied to seconds,
Hz, support booleans, activity counts or probability events. Rows are explicitly
data-limited or experimental, with owned gate
prerequisites. They are not an exhaustive all-band qualification claim. Remaining
frequency/jurisdiction, station/mode, mechanism-intersection and horizon expansions
require new versioned rows before implementation qualification. All validated
coverage counts are zero. 160 m remains a required dedicated engine; it is not
removed from scope by recording its current blocked state.

### A07/A23 numerical residual and convergence contract

For the analytic lossless/passive two-port fixtures, use unit incident time-averaged
normal Poynting flux: `F_inc=0.5 Re(E_inc cross H_inc*) dot n > 0`, with n into
the domain. R and T are outgoing reflected/transmitted flux magnitudes divided
by F_inc; A is integrated dissipated power divided by the same flux. Test
`abs(1-R-T-A)<=1e-6`, and R,T,A >= -1e-12 (roundoff diagnostic; negative physical
absorption is not clamped). Field-amplitude coefficients alone are not T without
impedance/angle normalization. For a reference complex solution use
`||u-u_ref||2/max(||u_ref||2,1e-12 U_inc)<=1e-6`, where fields have first been
nondimensionalized by declared incident E/H scales and U_inc is their unit norm.
A zero incident-flux, grazing-flux or singular normalization is unqualified.

For this candidate, a reference linear system with 2-norm condition number above
1e8 is outside the analytic relative-error gate; it is recorded as ill-conditioned,
not counted as passed. A family owner must preregister its independent condition
estimator and precision study before qualifying such cases. No global relaxed
residual is supplied here. At a coherent null `|H_ref|/|H_inc|<=1e-8`, test absolute
complex-amplitude error <=1e-6 |H_inc|; phase and dB relative error are undefined
and must be reported as such, never manufactured by a floor.

Away from nulls, halve spatial steps and separately double retained modes until
received level changes <0.1 dB and wrapped phase changes <1 degree. Two successive
successful refinements are necessary but not sufficient: an independent, justified
remaining-tail bound must also be below the same amplitude/phase tolerances. The
family owner must specify that bound (or reference comparison) before a gate can
pass. A stable last increment alone is not a tail estimate. The full-wave gate
remains BLOCKED: there is no production solver, conditioning study or tail proof.

## Adopted A01–A24 specification

### Propulse all-band propagation design — revision 2 extension

10 September 2026. Requested expansion of [epic #946](https://github.com/crypticpy/propulse/issues/946) and [mathematical specification #982](https://github.com/crypticpy/propulse/issues/982). M01–M24 remain the shared/HF mathematical contract. A01–A24 below extend it to 160 m, LF/MF, VHF/UHF, microwave/millimeter-wave and specialized amateur propagation. This is a concrete design and work plan, not a claim that those engines, observations, accuracy or independent reviews already exist.

**Product decision:** one application service with several physical engine families, a common station/channel interface and event-compatible learned adapters. 160 m is a required first expansion, not an optional out-of-range label. The other families have explicit implementation and measurement tasks. An unvalidated or data-limited capability remains visible as such and cannot be counted as completed scientific coverage.

#### 1. What “full coverage” means

**A01 — band × mechanism × event coverage.** A band name alone does not identify propagation physics. Record a capability for `(frequency range, mechanism, geometry, location/data domain, antenna/receiver class, output event, time horizon)`. Capability states are `planned`, `implemented_unvalidated`, `validated_climatology`, `validated_current`, `validated_forecast`, `data_limited`, and `unsupported`. Runtime input availability is separate from implementation/release status. A finite free-space calculation is not a validated terrestrial opening prediction.

The initial all-band RF inventory spans 2200 m through the nationally applicable millimeter bands up to 300 GHz. It includes 630 m, 160 m, all existing HF bands, 6 m, regional 8 m/4 m entries, 2 m, regional 1.25 m/33 cm entries, 70/23/13/9/5/3 cm, 24 and 47 GHz, and the applicable 76/122/134/241 GHz families. Exact allowed frequencies are versioned jurisdiction/license records, not universal allocations inferred from ITU region or a band nickname. Frequencies beyond the manifest and optical experiments are explicit future extensions, not hidden “covered” rows. The existing application array contains only eleven entries from 160 m through 6 m; it is not an all-band catalog, and its 6 m entry must not be described as HF.

“All-band numerical support,” “validated typical propagation,” and “validated specialized openings/forecasts” are separately scored milestones. Final benchmark coverage requires every agreed matrix row to meet its own evidence gate. A missing observation or rare-event corpus cannot be resolved by changing its status to “covered.”

**A02 — engine selection and channel contract.** Extend M01/M19 with `mechanismPolicy`, `geometryClass`, `terrainProfileId`, `environmentPackId`, `polarization`, `antennaHeightDatum`, and optional `relayOrTargetId/ephemerisId`. The router selects an eligible physical family before the online/offline fallback chain. ML may replace/augment a same-event head only inside its trained band/mechanism domain; existing NowCast/N5 does not acquire VHF/UHF/LF/space capabilities through this extension. Missing UHF inference falls back to UHF physics, never the HF engine.

Each family returns normalized channel components: transfer gain or basic transmission loss, delay/Doppler/spread, polarization transfer or its assumptions, path geometry, reference planes, uncertainty and a list of already included mechanisms/losses. HF reference statistical outputs remain separately named; do not invent complex phase from statistical power. Consumers ask the same service for the desired head and never infer “band open” from source counts or an unrelated mechanism.

**A03 — composition.** With the `exp(−iωt)` convention used throughout this extension, phase-resolved components at a common frequency have `H(f)=Σ a_j(f) exp[+i2πfτ_j]`, and received power is proportional to `|H|²`. Here τ_j represents the separately factored propagation delay; a_j contains remaining reflection/material phase. Derive total group delay from the complete H, rather than inserting that material delay into both factors. For unknown phase, sample the uncertain phase coherently within each realization; ensemble means of independent phases may approach `Σ|a_j|²`, but that is not a guarantee against a deep fade. A positive-power sum cannot represent destructive ground/sky interference.

Alternative complete models of the same mechanism are compared or selected, not added. P.2001 already combines its prescribed terrestrial mechanisms; do not append another complete diffraction/troposcatter loss to its result. An Earth–ionosphere mode solver containing the surface branch must not have P.368 surface power added again. Statistical time-percentile results do not supply independent simultaneous channel taps. Mixture/union probabilities need joint event definitions and shared latent state; no maximum-score cherry-picking. A directional receiving antenna and delayed/interfering modes use the same wanted-channel convention as M08/M10.

#### 2. Make 160 m and the lower bands scientifically real

**A04 — reference boundaries.** [P.368-10](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.368-10-202208-I/en) provides a 10 kHz–30 MHz ground-wave foundation. [P.1147-4](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.1147/en) supplies skywave field-strength predictions around 150–1700 kHz, so it does not directly certify 160 m, and its lower boundary also does not cover 2200 m. The selected P.533 reference starts at 2 MHz. There is therefore an actual 160 m reference gap. Do not hide it by clipping frequency or by blending out-of-domain recommendations and calling the result standard-compliant.

The chosen 160 m design is a physical complex-reflection E/F skywave solver plus a separately accounted finite-ground surface wave. The chosen LF long-range design uses Earth–ionosphere waveguide modes. Both consume the same low-frequency profiles, ground state, geomagnetic field, station and noise contract. P.1147 is an independent comparator only within its documented domain; legacy 160 m and explicit P.533 extrapolations may be experimental comparison arms, never ground truth.

**A05 — finite-conductivity ground.** Use the convention `exp(−iωt)`. The effective relative permittivity of conductive ground is `εg*=εr+iσg/(ωε0)`, where σg is S/m. Port the normative P.368 algorithm/reference component with exact inputs, notices, source/version hashes and official fixtures. Model land/sea segments with the recommendation's applicable mixed-path procedure; neither arithmetic-average conductivity nor arbitrary per-bounce constants are substitutes. Treat rough terrain, antenna height and ground-wave applicability separately; a method's frequency range does not remove its geometry restrictions.

A reflection coefficient is polarization/angle/material-dependent and belongs in a complex skywave/terrestrial field calculation. The normal-incidence dielectric fixture is `Γ=(1−sqrt(εg*))/(1+sqrt(εg*))`, using the passive root; oblique TE/TM reflection follows Maxwell boundary continuity. The receiving convention and basis determine its phase sign. Track surface-wave attenuation separately from antenna ground-system loss; poor ground can affect both, but they represent different physical locations. If ground conductivity is unknown, return declared dry/typical/wet or measured-source scenarios with uncertainty rather than assigning seawater everywhere.

**A06 — low-frequency profiles and tensor.** Bundle versioned Ne(h), collision frequency νe(h), temperatures/species where needed, and geomagnetic B(g,h,t). Use a pinned IRI/FIRI-based prior with its exact flags and neutral/collision parameter sources; audit every profile's units and valid altitude/time/index range. [IRI documents FIRI as a distinct lower-ionosphere option](https://irimodel.org/iri2016_help.html); it must not be casually rescaled to match an unrelated E peak. The collision profile is an independent physical input, not deduced from foF2. Static/profile coefficient fitting is allowed as a documented local model component; no cloud inference is required at runtime.

With electron charge magnitude e, mass me, `C_B v=v×B` and electron velocity v, solve `[(νe−iω)I+(e/me)C_B]v=−(e/me)E`. Conductivity tensor is `Σ=(Ne e²/me)[(νe−iω)I+(e/me)C_B]^-1`, and `εr*=I+iΣ/(ε0ω)`. Additional species are included by their charge/mass/collision response only when needed by the declared domain. This gives magnetization, collisions and absorption in one wave equation; it avoids replacing all lower-ionosphere physics with a secant law. Solve the linear system numerically rather than explicitly inverting an ill-conditioned matrix.

In the unmagnetized limit, `εr*=1−ωp²/[ω(ω+iνe)]`, `ωp²=Ne e²/(meε0)`. Verify zero-density vacuum, collisionless dispersion and passive absorption. For scalar `n=n'+in''`, amplitude decays as `exp(−k0 n''s)` and attenuation is `8.685889638 × k0 n''s` dB. Do not multiply by an extra factor of two: that constant already converts power loss from the complex field exponent. Tensor energy tests use Poynting flux, not a scalar |n| proxy.

**A07 — stable ionospheric reflection.** At each frequency and horizontal wavevector, solve the stratified Maxwell boundary problem for tangential `[Ex,Ey,Hx,Hy]` with passive/radiating upper conditions and finite-conductivity lower conditions. Integrate invariant-embedding/Riccati reflection or compose stable layer scattering matrices; do not use an unscaled transfer-matrix product through strongly evanescent layers. The result is a 2×2 reflection/Jones operator with phase, amplitude and polarization conversion. Refine vertical layers until reflection power and phase converge. A transmission/reflection energy budget must be consistent with collisional dissipation.

[LongwaveModePropagator](https://fgasdia.github.io/LongwaveModePropagator.jl/stable/) is a useful open methodological and independent numerical comparison for its documented VLF cases. Its existence is not evidence that its implementation is already accurate or efficient at 135 kHz, 475 kHz or 1.9 MHz. Our larger-frequency extension needs independent convergence and field measurements at each target band. The production portable kernel is isolated from any Julia runtime; implementation-language/asset licensing is checked before selecting source reuse. Do not download or wrap a restricted operational model as a hidden dependency.

**A08 — 160 m circuit calculation.** For every candidate E/F eigenpath, calculate spherical geometry and launch/arrival elevation, geometric spreading from neighboring-ray/ray-tube derivatives, antenna coupling, and the ordered ground/ionosphere Jones matrices. A schematic channel is `H_j=C_j × J_rx [Π_k R_g,k R_i,k P_k] J_tx × exp(iφ_j)`, with free-space/geometric spreading C_j and propagation operators P_k expressed at declared planes. The actual traversal order follows the path; no omitted ground reflection at an intermediate hop and no final fictitious ground bounce before the receiving antenna.

For the first portable implementation, build a stratified spherical, horizontally segmented E/F model with a converged elevation/eigenpath search. Reflection phase contributes group delay through `τ=+d arg H/dω` under the delay convention of A03; store the complete signed phase convention in fixtures. An isolated pure delay must reproduce its positive delay, but interference/dispersive group delay is not necessarily positive and is undefined at channel zeros. Use phase unwrapping with conditioning checks; do not clamp negative or singular composite group delay into a fictitious path travel time. Use uniform/finite-aperture treatment or a verified wave solver near caustics: divergent ray-tube gain is not a real infinite signal. Where asymptotics do not converge, label that head unavailable and use a qualified lower-resolution solver/scenario, not a clipped “excellent” score. Combine distinct ground/sky components using A03 when phase support exists, otherwise give distribution/scenario results.

Full-wave collision absorption already responds to the profile. Do not add the M15 complete D-RAP/P.533 absorption again. The reflection operator is defined at a fixed matching surface and includes propagation above that surface; P_k and geometric phase include only traversal not already in that operator. Live solar/proton/ionospheric observations may change a validated profile-state estimate with explicit altitude/error operators; unsupported spectral information remains advisory. Unknown proton spectrum is not invented from one channel. Short/night/terminator/ocean/polar cases need separate evidence; directional polarization through a magnetized medium must not be forced into an unqualified reciprocal scalar channel.

**A09 — LF/MF waveguide.** Solve the modal dispersion/boundary problem from A07 plus ground reflection, retaining enough modes for amplitude/phase convergence. An isotropic parallel-plate fixture satisfies `1−r_g r_i exp(2ikzH)=0`; the coupled polarization case uses the corresponding matrix determinant. Propagate segment modes with complex horizontal wavenumber and spherical geometry, including mode conversion at environmental transitions: schematically `E(s)=Σ a_n(s) exp[i∫ k_h,n(s')ds']`. Launch and receive coupling use the actual antenna currents/effective heights. Do not add a second surface-wave branch if the modal solution already includes it.

The 2200 m/630 m module must test day/night transition, mixed ground, mode-search completeness, and higher-frequency mode counts rather than inherit the VLF model's default search mesh. Keep P.1147 field-strength comparisons distinct from phase-coherent mode predictions. An independent smooth-earth/full-wave reference and characterized beacon/controlled measurements are needed before validated LF skywave claims. Ground-wave coverage can qualify first without declaring the entire LF row complete.

**A10 — low-band station quality.** Delivered RF power, ERP/EIRP, radiated power, antenna efficiency, matching loss and ground-system resistance are different quantities. A declared antenna model has `ηrad=Rrad/(Rrad+Rloss)` and realized gain from its accepted pattern/mismatch model. Do not count this efficiency twice when a measured realized-gain pattern already includes it. Electrically short LF/MF antennas require measured or independently modeled current distribution/efficiency; wavelength-based “quarter-wave gain” is not a substitute.

At LF/HF the receiver may be dominated by atmospheric/man-made noise and impulsive interference, with direction-dependent receive antennas; M09's scalar external noise is only its declared equivalent-temperature representation. Preserve bandwidth/detector/measurement support. A receive loop that rejects local noise must have both signal pattern and noise-environment coupling represented. Absolute station SNR cannot be calibrated from unknown antenna/power WSPR reports without the M16 selection/station treatment.

#### 3. VHF/UHF, microwave and millimeter-wave terrestrial engines

**A11 — terrain geometry and data.** Terrain profiles use geodesic surface distance, geodetic coordinates and explicit orthometric/ellipsoidal conversions; antenna height AGL is not altitude AMSL. Sample at sufficient resolution for the terrain and Fresnel geometry, preserving DEM voids and vertical error. The first Fresnel radius at a point is `r1=sqrt(λ d1 d2/(d1+d2))`. The spherical effective-Earth bulge fixture is `b=d1 d2/(2kR)`. A single knife edge has `v=h sqrt[2(d1+d2)/(λ d1d2)]`; use the selected [P.526 diffraction method](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.526/en) for complete terrain, not a sum of isolated knife-edge losses.

Pin DEM/surface/clutter/ground/coast data rights, resolution, vertical datum and asset hashes. Offer validated regional offline packs; keep existing map-rendering elevation sources separate from radio-quality profiles. Global coarse data can provide broad scenarios but cannot prove a ridge-free microwave path. Missing terrain is `data_limited`: provide labeled free-space/earth-curvature diagnostics and uncertainty, never a falsely validated unobstructed path. A path outside an installed pack must remain responsive offline and show the exact missing capability. Detailed global terrain cannot realistically be assumed bundled in a small application.

**A12 — terrestrial climatological reference.** Use a pinned, faithful [P.2001-6](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.2001/en) implementation and normative coefficient/test package for its 30 MHz–50 GHz domain, with all subsidiary method editions recorded. The latest standalone diffraction edition is not silently swapped into a different reference algorithm. Explicitly reconcile the documented P.2001-5 troposcatter defect before porting; do not accidentally ship a superseded component. Validate native/portable intermediate geometry, diffraction, scatter and combined output. [NTIA ITM](https://its.ntia.gov/software/itm/) is a useful separate comparator on its 20 MHz–20 GHz domain, not an out-of-domain millimeter reference.

Its time-percentage/exceedance outputs describe statistical populations, not tomorrow's exact hourly opening probability. Preserve that event meaning in the offline head. M24 replay and live enhancements may support a forecast head after separate conditioning/validation. Endpoint antenna gains, clutter and feeder losses are applied exactly once according to each reference implementation's contract. Standard basic-transmission-loss output is not automatically a complete station C/N result.

**A13 — observed/forecast refractivity.** For the declared meteorological approximation, pressure p_d and water-vapor partial pressure e in hPa, temperature T in K, `N=77.6 p_d/T+72 e/T+3.75×10^5 e/T²`, `n=1+10^-6 N`. Use the precise chosen [P.453](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.453/en) formulation and validity for production. Modified refractivity `M=N+10^6 h/R` (h,R same units) reveals trapping candidates where dM/dh<0. The constant-gradient approximation gives `k=[1+R×10^-6 dN/dh]^-1` with h in meters; singular/nonpositive k is a warning that the approximation failed, not permission to clamp a huge radio horizon.

Obtain pressure/temperature/humidity/geopotential-height profiles at their actual vertical resolution; surface humidity/temperature alone do not define an elevated duct. Normalize issued forecasts with M02. [NOAA NOMADS](https://nomads.ncep.noaa.gov/) provides GFS/GEFS products, but neither their availability nor a coarse pressure grid proves resolution of a shallow marine duct. Inspect exact variables, model levels, cadence and capture rights before activation. Use qualified regional model-level/radiosonde products where available. Numerical weather prediction is environmental input, not Propulse cloud propagation inference; the local physics still runs if its own ML service is absent. Do not choose NOAA's AI weather products as an undisclosed “physics-only” dependency.

**A14 — current/forecast anomalous propagation.** Add a separately versioned terrain/refractivity field solver for suitable two-dimensional profiles, with baseline/correction ownership distinct from P.2001's total statistics. The scalar narrow-angle parabolic-equation candidate is `∂ψ/∂x = i/(2k0) ∂²ψ/∂z² + i k0(n_eff²−1)ψ/2`, with the chosen Earth-flattening, impedance boundary and absorbing upper boundary. Use a stable split-step scheme, terrain treatment and mesh refinement; forward narrow-angle PE does not represent arbitrary backscatter, large-angle turns or all polarization effects.

Run the same solver over joint meteorological ensembles or calibrated profile uncertainty for future windows. A qualified ray solution may accelerate ordinary cases; caustics/ducts need verified wave treatment rather than capped ray gain. If the mesh/domain or input profile is inadequate, use the qualified climatological head and label the missing event-specific forecast. Do not add PE total path loss to P.2001 total path loss. Cache profiles/transfer results by frequency, terrain, atmosphere, antenna and solver version; do not linearly interpolate across unresolved duct resonances. This enhanced solver is optional for an initial terrestrial release, but required before the relevant detailed duct-forecast row can pass.

**A15 — microwave/millimeter line-of-sight.** Above 50 GHz, do not extrapolate P.2001. For independently verified line-of-sight geometry, calculate spreading, polarization, pointing and path-integrated material attenuation: `L=20log10(4πd/λ)+∫[γgas(f,p,T,e)+γrain(f,Rrain,pol)+γcloud(f,Lwater)]ds+Lpoint+Lother`, in consistent dB/km and km. Use the line-by-line [P.676-13 gas method](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.676-13-202208-I!!PDF-E.pdf) in its domain, the selected [P.838 rain coefficients](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.838/en) with `γrain=k_r Rrain^α`, and [P.840 cloud liquid-water treatment](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.840/en). Each submethod's own frequency/temperature/domain limits remain enforced. Weather layers are spatially integrated, not a city-wide rain badge converted into a global penalty.

For climate availability use applicable [P.530](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.530/en) procedures within their defined geometry/domain; do not substitute an annual rain-exceedance percentage for a 24-hour nowcast. A partly obstructed path needs a validated diffraction/reflection/scatter treatment, not a free-space result labeled complete. Above the qualified terrestrial-scatter domain, return explicit limits. Pointing uncertainty and antenna-aperture far-field validity matter increasingly with frequency; near-field installations need a separate supported coupling model.

**A16 — atmospheric noise and wideband receiver.** Atmospheric attenuation also emits noise. For a uniform passive layer, `T_out=T_in exp(−τ)+J_f(Tlayer)[1−exp(−τ)]`, where `τ=A_dB/4.342944819` and `J_f(T)=(hf/kB)/(exp(hf/(kBT))−1)` is Rayleigh–Jeans-equivalent brightness. Use the stable `expm1` form. The h→0/low-frequency limit is T, but the cosmic background at high millimeter frequencies cannot always be represented by the classical 2.7 K approximation. Beam-average sky, Sun/Moon, atmosphere, ground spillover and antenna losses in the declared calibration convention. Preserve measured receiver-system noise instead of adding modeled components again.

Polarization coupling is `ηpol=|u_rx† J_pol u_tx|²` for normalized transmit/receive Jones vectors and the polarization operator with already-accounted bulk attenuation removed; `Lpol=−10log10ηpol` is its scalar-coupling diagnostic. If using the complete lossy channel Jones matrix instead, its projected power already includes those losses and receives no second scalar attenuation/polarization addition. A genuine orthogonal null is not repaired with a generic 3 dB assumption. Linear/circular handedness, basis direction and Faraday non-reciprocity need explicit fixtures. Directional source/noise patterns and terminal reference planes flow into M09/M10. Mode profiles now include FM/AM and specialized weak-signal modes such as Q65 and MSK144 with their actual decoder durations, spread tolerances, capture/interference semantics and bandwidths. An HF FT8 threshold cannot supply all-band decoder performance.

#### 4. Specialized propagation families

**A17 — sporadic E.** Implement [P.534-6](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/p/R-REC-P.534-6-202109-I!!PDF-E.pdf) as a separately named statistical Es head with its prescribed foEs distributions and geometry, not an extra Kp multiplier. Its documented data and formula domains include latitude, distance, hop and f/foEs restrictions; validate those exact predicates in the adapter. Do not treat foF2 as foEs. Regular-layer HF power and an alternative complete Es estimate cannot be blindly summed, and the method is not a universal TEP model. Physical MF/HF critical frequency, sporadic-E blanketing and cloud extent remain distinct observations.

For a current Es extension, represent patches with location/extent, height, foEs or a supported equivalent parameter and their covariance. Eligible reports constrain feasible reflection regions/thresholds; they do not uniquely reconstruct a global Es sheet. Use a censored/exposure-aware observation operator and shared patch uncertainty, retaining source restrictions from M11/M16. Patch persistence/advection and emergence are fit to independent sequences; without validated transitions return dated evidence plus climatology, not confident exact openings 24 hours ahead. No modern same-day global Es map is assumed to exist in the current collectors.

**A18 — high-frequency F2 / TEP / off-great-circle rays.** The new three-dimensional magnetoionic research track consumes a spatial ionosphere/tensor from A06 and solves eigenrays between endpoints. For dispersion branch D(x,k,ω)=0, Hamiltonian rays obey `dx/ds=∂D/∂k`, `dk/ds=−∂D/∂x` up to a consistent path parameter; derive travel time and amplitude from the same dispersion/transport system. Account for Earth curvature, magnetic field, gradients, polarization and collisions. A scalar Chapman/secant extension cannot certify 6 m F2/TEP or an off-great-circle path.

Use symplectic/adaptive integration, endpoint shooting, branch tracking and convergence to a stratified reference; represent caustics and mode coupling with wave/uniform treatment or explicit unavailability. Smooth median IRI state does not supply equatorial irregularities or guarantee a TEP event. Qualify daytime/refraction and evening/irregularity-dependent phenomena as distinct rows; irregularity spectra/occurrence need measured parameterizations and held-out evidence. This track is required for those claimed capabilities, but it does not block the earlier 160 m engine or ordinary terrain-aware VHF release.

**A19 — auroral and other bistatic scatter.** An auroral oval is context, not a radio reflector with known gain. Find mutually visible, field-aligned scattering volumes and evaluate aspect angle, antenna patterns, absorption and spectrum at the actual scattering vector q=k_s−k_i. A general single-scatter mean-power diagnostic is `Pr = Pt λ²/(4π)^3 ∫ [Gt(r)Gr(r) η(r,q,pol)/(Rt(r)² Rr(r)²)] exp(−τt−τr) dV`. Define η=dσ_b/dV in m^-1, with bistatic radar cross-section σ_b=4π dσ/dΩ; τt/τr are power optical depths. This integral assumes incoherent volume contributions; coherent targets require field composition. Its amplitude/spread requires a calibrated irregularity spectrum; optical brightness or Kp alone does not identify η. If not identifiable, return geometric opportunity/qualified activity rather than a fabricated SNR.

The same geometric integration infrastructure can support rain scattering with a validated droplet/scattering model and radar-derived volume, or a discrete aircraft target with bistatic radar cross-section. These remain different registered mechanisms and different parameter sources. A weather-radar reflectivity product cannot be inserted as microwave communication cross-section without the conversion/model assumptions. Aircraft trajectories need qualified fresh state/prediction uncertainty, and unknown cross-section/orientation remains a scenario. Statistical offline traffic/rain/aurora opportunity is distinct from predicting a particular future scatterer.

**A20 — meteor scatter.** Use [P.843](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.843/en) as a documented meteor-burst foundation and validate modern decoder/channel assumptions separately. Calculate transmitter/receiver visibility of a trail, specular/aspect geometry, frequency-dependent scattering, duration and delay/Doppler spread. A candidate opportunity process has intensity `λ_eff(t)=∫ rate(z,t) P(trail detectable and usable | z,station,mode) dz`; under a validated conditional Poisson model, `P(at least one usable burst in [t0,t1])=1−exp(−∫λ_eff dt)`. This is a burst event, not a completed two-way exchange. Shower streams can be clustered, requiring a Cox/overdispersed model if the Poisson diagnostics fail.

Offline solar-time/season/shower-radiant climatology is useful but must carry its date, reference population and uncertainty. Current meteor observations constrain rates with source exposure and latency; visual meteor counts are not a universal radio echo count. A protocol-state simulation with actual burst lengths, transmit/listen periods and decoder duration is required to estimate a complete exchange. Without those calibrated quantities, show opportunity and expected waiting-time scenarios, not a precise QSO percentage.

**A21 — satellites and relays.** Reuse existing `satellite.js`/SGP4 and satellite catalog ownership, including the orbit work in [#994](https://github.com/crypticpy/propulse/issues/994). Do not build a competing map/orbit store. Verify the chosen propagator against [CelesTrak's SGP4 reference procedure](https://www.celestrak.org/software/tutorials/sgp4-verification.php), with frame/time conventions, observer rotation and ephemeris age explicit. A propagated element set without error calibration is not a guaranteed precise pass; stale/maneuvered/decayed objects must degrade the pass head. Offline operation can use qualified cached elements but cannot promise indefinite accurate satellite predictions without updates.

For a path leg, `τ=ρ/c`, `Δf≈−f ρdot/c` (positive receding range rate). Evaluate uplink and downlink separately with actual satellite/transponder antennas, polarization, receiver noise, frequency conversion and power sharing/backoff. A linear bent-pipe AWGN diagnostic at common bandwidth has `1/(C/N)_total=1/(C/N)_up+1/(C/N)_down+1/(C/I)_other`; this does not describe a regenerative digital relay, whose conditional packet/decode stages must be modeled separately. Mutual footprint visibility does not establish transponder availability or operator completion. [P.619](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.619/en) supplies relevant space/Earth path treatment within its own scope, not a complete amateur transponder model.

Offline ground-repeater/relay scenarios can also compose two qualified legs using a cached or user-specified relay configuration. Reachability and service uptime/access are separate events; do not assume an unverified repeater is on air. Preserve individual leg event/uncertainty and conditioning instead of multiplying unrelated display scores.

**A22 — Earth–Moon–Earth.** Use a bundled, range-limited lunar ephemeris with validated interpolation and Earth orientation/time transformations; compare against [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html). The Moon has predictable orbital geometry without a live propagation service, but coefficient coverage and terrestrial time accuracy remain explicit. Evaluate both stations' visibility, terrain horizon, bistatic light time and Doppler, pointing/polarization, sky/Moon/Sun noise, libration-driven spreading and actual decoder duration.

The bistatic radar budget is `Pr=Pt Gt Gr λ² σmoon/[(4π)^3 Rt² Rr² L]`, with SI distances, a declared effective lunar scattering cross-section and all other losses in linear power factor L. σmoon is frequency/polarization/geometry-dependent and must be independently characterized; a single assumed lunar albedo is not a validated all-band constant. Delay is approximately `(Rt+Rr)/c` with moving-body/light-time refinement for precision, and Doppler uses both leg range rates. A free-space Earth–Moon distance loss alone misses the scattering loss. Use [WSJT-X astronomical/echo quantities](https://wsjt.sourceforge.io/wsjtx-main_en.html) as independent operational comparisons for delay, Doppler/spread, sky temperature and degradation on compatible assumptions. Offline EME opportunity is therefore possible, but personal success requires the complete station and scattering/noise model.
