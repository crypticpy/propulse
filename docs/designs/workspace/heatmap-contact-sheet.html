Band × Continent heat map — design contact sheet

One widget, four densities. Rows are bands, columns are continents, the number is the deduplicated spot count in the window, and the tint is the Band Health ladder verdict for that path from your grid — so a cell can carry a big number and still be closed to you. Fill opacity is traffic and hue is the verdict, at every density — quiet cells are translucent, hot cells are solid, and a hot cell your station can't work keeps its colour under a hatch. Selecting a band or a cell writes band + continent to shared operating state; the spot list, the map and the logger read it back. Every density below is a scale range, not a fixed size: the pixel dimensions named are the point in the range being drawn.

3b · Mode: my footprintwork density, small end of the range · "where am I being spotted"

The Mode segment flips the same matrix from "who is being heard by anyone" to "who is hearing me". Same rows, same columns, same encoding — but the numbers are receptions of my own transmissions, so the matrix is an order of magnitude sparser and normalises to its own maximum (34, not 386). That is the honest comparison: my 34 into NA is a solid cell because it is my best path, not because it rivals the world's traffic. Shown here as a separate panel so both modes can be compared side by side; in the product it is one panel with a toggle.

Notes for reviewquestions this mock raises
  1. Resolved in v2 — recorded here so the decision is not re-litigated. Cells now carry one saturated ramp at every density (opacity = traffic, hue = verdict). The hot-but-unworkable case keeps its heat colour with a hatch laid over it, labelled "your station can't work this" at work density and left unlabelled on the wall. Wall click opens a report; workstation click performs the filter action. Each density is a continuous scale range, not a fixed size.
  2. One verdict vocabulary, or two? The brief says open / marginal / closed; the shipped ladder is closed < forecast < stirring < verified open < hot with an orthogonal surprise flag (src/lib/verdict/ladder.ts). This mock uses the shipped five so the heat map, Best Band Now and the Home ladder cannot disagree. Confirm — or define the three-state reduction once, centrally, and let every surface use it.
  3. Is the opacity ramp linear or logarithmic? Drawn linear here against a 400-spot ceiling, which makes 20 m × NA (386) and 20 m × EU (274) look nearly identical and flattens everything under 40 spots into a wash. A log ramp would separate the quiet end, which is where openings start — but it also makes the wall harder to read at a glance, because the eye reads area and opacity linearly. Also unresolved: is the ceiling fixed at 400, or the current maximum (so the picture rescales every refresh)?
  4. What counts as "can't work this"? The hatch fires on band capability AND Band Health. Band capability is knowable (antenna and power from the station profile). Band Health from-my-grid is a model output with a confidence. Should a low-confidence "closed" hatch a cell at all, or only a high-confidence one? Hatching a cell the operator could actually work is the worse error — it tells them not to try.
  5. Does a cell click filter, or filter and open the report? Section 4 makes the workstation click an action (filter, no modal) and the wall click a report. That split is deliberate but it means the same widget behaves differently in two placements. Confirm that is wanted, or add a modifier (long-press / secondary target) so both are reachable everywhere.
  6. Which headline rule ships as the default? Section 3b shows the three candidates disagreeing on the same data: hottest cell says 20 m → EU, newest opening says 15 m → AS, best workable says work 20 m → EU. "My best workable band" is the only one that can never name a path the operator can't take, which argues for it as the default — but it is also the least interesting sentence on a club wall where the operators are not all the same station.
  7. Does band visibility follow the operator or the placement? Section 3c hides 12 / 10 / 6 m per user. If that follows the operator, the wall in a club shack shows one member's band set to everyone. If it follows the placement, an operator has to set it three times (wall, desk, phone). A third option: station-profile-derived (hide bands with no antenna) with a per-placement override.
  8. Does "My footprint" need its own ladder? Footprint counts are receptions of me, so "verified open" and "hot" are measuring a different thing — one loud reporter can carry a cell. Either the ladder thresholds change in footprint mode, or footprint drops the verdict hue entirely and uses a single tone with opacity only. The mock currently reuses the verdict hues, which may be quietly dishonest.
  9. Window choice: 30 min / 2 h — or does the verdict window differ from the count window? The ladder's verified bar is defined on a trailing 20 minutes (VERIFIED_OBS_ENTER, 6 obs / 3 reporters). If the operator selects a 2 h count window, the tint is still describing 20 minutes. Either the ladder window follows the selector, or the footer has to say plainly that the number and the colour are measured over different spans.
  10. Phone in World scope. Page 3 collapses to a band ladder because the scope is "my continent". In World scope the phone needs either a horizontally paged matrix (6 columns will not fit at a 44 px target), a continent picker above the ladder, or the World scope disabled on phones altogether. (Phone canvas is unchanged from v1.)
