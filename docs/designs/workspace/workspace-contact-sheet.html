Workspace — design contact sheet

Widgets are nouns, workflows are verbs. A workspace is the container. It is instantiated on one of four canvas types — PHONE · TABLET · WORKSTATION · WALL — and the canvas type decides the rules, not the operator. A workspace is made of two things: configurable rails and a space (the hero area). Widgets are chosen from the picker and auto-dock into the rails or the space by the canvas's own rules; there is no manual slot placement. A workspace holds several pages the operator flips through with the same pager idiom the wall already uses.

Everything drawn below is assembled from components that already exist in the design system — the HamClock wall tiles, the solar metric cards and charts, the DX spot list, the NowCast band panel, the propagation forecast, the HamClock dialog and its segmented controls. Nothing in today's app changes. This sheet is the design vehicle, not a build plan: it exists to be read next to heatmap-contact-sheet.html, from which it takes its tokens, its encoding and its voice.

0 · Canvas typesone workspace concept · four instantiations

The same workspace definition — the same pages, the same widget list — lands differently on each canvas because each canvas declares its own rails, its own scale range and its own target floor. A widget added on the workstation appears on the phone as a page; a widget added on the phone appears on the wall in a rail. The operator never places a widget in a slot. They add it to a page and the layout engine docks it.

Review noteswhat this mock asserts, what it leaves open
  1. Asserted, not open: one registry, four densities. Section 1 is the load-bearing claim — every widget family already has a home at wall/glance/work/phone, or a named gap, without inventing a second component set. The heat map is the only widget with no existing form anywhere.
  2. Open — does a page pin to the workspace, or roam with the operator? A workspace is per-screen; pages are the operator's tools. If pages roam, the club shack's wall flips to whatever page someone built on their phone; if they pin, the same page has to be rebuilt on every screen. WORKSPACE-CONCEPT.md §10 proposes pin-by-default, recipes roam — this mock draws pinned pages (section 2) and does not draw the roaming case. Confirm.
  3. Open — what does auto-dock do when a rail is already full? Section 3's picker assumes an empty slot. Refuse the add with a spelled-out reason, evict the lowest-weight widget to another rail, or spill onto a new page automatically — refusing is the most honest and the least surprising, but the picker then has to explain itself in one sentence the operator can act on. Not drawn here.
  4. Open — does band visibility follow the operator or the placement? Section 4's DISPLAY tab sets it per workspace. Following the operator instead means one club member's band set reaches every screen in the shack; a third option derives it from the station profile (hide bands with no antenna) with a per-workspace override. Undecided.
  5. Open — wall click opens a report, workstation click performs an action. Section 2/3 draw the workstation click as an action (filter, no modal) — the same split heatmap-contact-sheet.html section 4 draws for the wall vs. workstation heat map. WORKSPACE-CONCEPT.md §4 resolves it by putting "Open report" in every widget header regardless of canvas; confirm that is sufficient, or a modifier (long-press / secondary target) is needed so both stay reachable everywhere.
  6. Open — default headline rule, and does the phone get World scope? Section 4 shows "Hottest cell" selected with "My best workable band" as the alternative that can never name an unworkable path — the least interesting sentence on a club wall but the most honest one on a personal workstation. Separately, a phone at 44 pt cannot fit six columns in World scope; section 5 only draws "my continent." A horizontally paged matrix, a continent picker above the ladder, or disabling World scope on phones are the three options — none drawn.
  7. Blocking — the heat-map encoding itself is two different pictures. This mock's heat-map miniatures (section 1, and the transposed/natural grids in sections 2–3) use the v2 hue-by-verdict, opacity-by-traffic ramp from heatmap-contact-sheet.html — hue = Band Health ladder state, opacity = raw count against a 400-spot ceiling. Audit §16, written later the same day, decided per-cell baseline normalisation instead: a log2 ratio against the same-UTC-hour baseline, drawn on a diverging quiet-grey / workable-green / crowded-orange ramp, with an open sub-question of whether "crowded" also needs an absolute floor (ratio > 2× and count ≥ 10). These are not reconcilable by drawing harder — PR 1 in WORKSPACE-CONCEPT.md §9 (heat map compute + baseline) cannot start until the owner picks one.
  8. Scope decision made in this mock, stated here rather than left silent. Section 2 draws recipes A and B as full pixel frames and C/D as placement tables, to keep this sheet a reasonable size — not because C/D are less real. Recipe D explicitly depends on the operating-session object (WORKSPACE-CONCEPT.md §5, "ships last") so a lighter-weight treatment there also reflects its actual build order.
  9. Non-goals this mock does not draw, on purpose. No engine work — the Opportunity object and the callsign-activity aggregate stay out of scope; the registry only has to be able to render one when it exists. No free window manager and no manual slot placement — every "move" action in section 3 is MOVE TO RAIL, never drag-to-slot. Nothing about Home, PropSphere, the wall or Solar Pulse changes; every visual here is a facsimile, not a modified real component.
  10. DS components reused, by section. §1 — HamClockTile/wall-tile grammar, PrimaryMetrics, MetricCard, SolarSeriesChart, DXSpotList/SpotRow, NowCastBandPanel, BandActivityBar, HomeBandsLadder, the DS-15 SectionHeader band (su-rule/su-band). §2 — WorkspaceCanvas/WorkspaceBar/SpaceSlot/RailSlot composition shapes (proposed, §9), HamClockPager footer idiom, hero-switch radiogroup pattern. §3 — HamClockToggleRow-style spelled toggles, the centre-overlay picker grammar from heatmap-contact-sheet.html §4/§3c. §4 — HamClockDialog, HamClockTabs, HamClockToggleRow, HamClockSegmented, theme cards from the wall settings ThemeTab. §5 — MobileLayout phone shell grammar, PathAnalysis/#624 Decision-layer shape, the state-strip idiom carried from every wall header.
